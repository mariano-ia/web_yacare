How Much an AI Product Costs: A Real Guide

An honest breakdown of what you pay when building a product with artificial intelligence: team, models, infrastructure and maintenance, with ranges and the levers that move your budget.

R
Rafa Guerrero June 11, 2026 8 min read

Why Nobody Gives You an Upfront Number

When you ask how much it costs to build an AI product, the honest answer starts with another question: what problem do you want to solve, and for how many users? The cost of an internal assistant for ten people looks nothing like an app that handles thousands of queries a day. So be skeptical of any fixed quote before the scope is defined.

In this article we split the cost into four blocks: team, AI models, infrastructure and maintenance. The goal is for you to understand where the money goes and, above all, which of your decisions move the needle. We do not sell magic: we sell clarity so you can put together an MVP budget that adds up.

One important point before we go on: most of the cost of an AI product is not the AI. It is the software around it, the integrations, the interface and the validation work. The model is usually a smaller slice of the total.

The Four Cost Blocks

Team. This is almost always the biggest expense. For an MVP you need, at a minimum, development, product design and someone who understands applied AI. Depending on seniority and region, a small team working for a few weeks accounts for most of your initial budget.

AI models. Here you have two paths. Using a third party API (like those from OpenAI, Anthropic or Google) has zero startup cost and you pay per use. Training or fine tuning your own model costs far more in time and compute, and it rarely makes sense for an MVP. To start, the API is almost always the way to go.

Infrastructure. Hosting, database, vector storage if you use semantic search, and monitoring. In the early stage this is modest, but it grows with user volume and with the amount of data you store and query.

Maintenance. The cost almost everyone forgets. An AI product is never finished: models change, prompts degrade, edge cases show up and things need adjusting. Set aside a recurring budget for this from day one.

Realistic Ranges for an MVP

Let's talk numbers, with the caveat that they depend on scope. A prototype to validate an idea, with no code or very little, can cost very little if you build it with existing tools. To see how, check out validating a product idea with AI without code.

A functional MVP ready for your first users, with a tightly scoped AI feature, usually lands somewhere from several thousand to tens of thousands of dollars depending on complexity, integrations and team. The difference between the floor and the ceiling is almost always scope, not technology.

API usage cost depends on traffic. For an MVP with hundreds of users and moderate queries, it is usually a small monthly figure next to the cost of the team. It only becomes relevant when you scale, and by then you already have revenue or learning that justifies it.

The Levers You Control

Scope. This is the number one lever. Every extra feature adds design, development, testing and maintenance. An MVP that does one thing well costs a fraction of one that tries to do five things halfway.

Build versus buy. Before writing code, ask yourself whether an existing tool solves 80 percent of the problem. Sometimes an automation built with ready made services is enough to validate. If you run an SMB, starting with an AI automation is usually cheaper than building from scratch.

Type of AI. Using a general model via API is cheap and fast. Training your own is expensive and slow, and it only makes sense once you have validated demand and need something no general model can give you. In an MVP, that need almost never exists yet.

Data quality. If your data is messy, you will pay in hours of cleanup and prep. Tidy data makes everything that follows cheaper.

When Building Is Not Worth the Spend

There are cases where the honest answer is not to build yet. If you have not validated that someone has the problem and would pay to solve it, a full MVP is premature. Better to invest a little in a discovery sprint or a no code test.

It also makes sense to hold off if the problem is solved by an existing tool. Not everything needs a custom product. A chatbot, for example, is not always the best option: we explain it in when a chatbot is worth it and when it isn't.

And if your team is still learning what generative AI can do, it is worth aligning expectations first. That is what generative AI for product teams is for. Spending money on unvalidated assumptions is the most common way to burn through a budget.

If you do not have a technical team, you are not obligated to hire one before testing. In how to apply AI in an SMB without a technical team we show low cost paths to get started.

How to Build Your Budget Without Surprises

Start by writing down the single feature your product needs to be useful. Estimate the team and the weeks for that, add a margin for the unexpected (there always is some) and add a monthly line for model usage and infrastructure. Finally, set aside a recurring maintenance budget, ideally a percentage of the build cost.

Think in phases, not in one big launch. Validate cheaply, build the minimum, measure and only then decide where to invest more. This reduces the risk of spending a lot on something nobody uses.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an AI MVP cost for a startup?

It depends on scope, but a functional MVP with a tightly scoped AI feature usually lands between several thousand and tens of thousands of dollars. The difference comes from the number of features and integrations, not the AI technology itself. Validating first with a cheap prototype narrows that range significantly.

Is it better to use an AI API or train your own model?

For an MVP, a third party API is almost always the way to go: it has zero startup cost and you pay per use. Training your own model costs far more in time and compute, and it only makes sense once you have validated demand and need something no general model offers.

What share of the budget goes to the AI?

Less than most people imagine. The model cost is usually a smaller slice next to the team, design, integrations and maintenance. AI is one piece, not the bulk of the product.

How much does it cost to maintain an AI product?

Maintenance is a recurring expense many people forget. Models change, prompts degrade and new cases come up that need adjusting. It is wise to set aside a percentage of the build cost as a monthly or annual budget from the start.
